## Verification procedure for each open issue

1. Obtain exact versioned original from the publishing body. Record access date and stable URL; record full file hash if authorised to retain a copy.
2. Extract short source-supported propositions with exact locator and document status; do not copy lengthy source text.
3. Second checker tests fidelity and identifies what the statement **does not establish**.
4. Qualified CSO reviews any proposed hazard interpretation and alternate credible explanation, with status `reviewed` / `disputed` / `insufficient evidence`.
5. Rule candidate receives defined applicability boundary, positive and negative counterexamples, and evidence of useful detection before further promotion.
6. Record reviewer, date, signature or equivalent attributable authorisation and link to the versioned record **outside public GitHub if identifiable**.

## Critical prohibitions

- A web search snippet is **not** whole-document verification.
- Two AI-model outputs agreeing is **not** independent expert source adjudication.
- A real-world service evaluation is **not** a historical reconstruction of risk acceptance or patient outcomes.
- A DCB clause is not a universal numerical risk matrix and does not permit automated deployment approval.
- A case authored by the researcher is not an independent gold-standard validation case.
- No material may be marked **approved for clinical use** without independent, documented professional review.

They should independently check the selected DCB0129/DCB0160 clauses (CL-001–CL-008 and CL-017–CL-018), including DCB0129 §6.3.1's exception for controls implemented by another organisation, before any clause-driven professional warning.

A qualified CSO should then assess **causal transferability and counterexamples**, particularly:

- Does GOSH qualitative evidence reasonably prompt local review of clinically consequential documentation errors without implying a measured event rate?
- Which HSSIB e-RS mechanism is genuinely transferable as a *systems-safety analytical pattern*, and where does its analogy to AVT break?
- Which AVT guidance clauses are recommendatory guidance versus versioned DCB normative requirements, and what local assurance evidence would be needed?
- Under DCB0129/DCB0160 §§6.1.2–6.1.3, how should the method challenge hazards *introduced by controls* without assuming that the presence of a new control always increases risk?
- Under DCB0160 §2.2.2, how should professional review distinguish top-management risk acceptance and deployment authorisation from CSO document approval?
- Would each of the seven candidate challenge patterns create meaningful, non-duplicative findings under a fully controlled scenario? What evidence would make them false alarms?
